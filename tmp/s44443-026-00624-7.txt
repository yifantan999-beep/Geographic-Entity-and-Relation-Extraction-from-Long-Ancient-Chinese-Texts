Abstract
Document-level joint entity and relation extraction faces persistent challenges, including error propagation across sub-tasks, 
limited relational reasoning over long documents, and inadequate modeling of heterogeneous semantic edges. To address 
these issues, we propose a table-filling based multi-task explicit interaction framework. Mentions are uniformly represented 
as graph nodes, where coreference and relation graphs are dynamically constructed, and mention-level syntactic dependen-
cies are incorporated as structural priors. To further explore potential correlations between sub-tasks, we design an align-
ment graph between coreference resolution and relation extraction, explicitly modeling structural dependencies to enable 
bidirectional information flow and reinforce semantic complementarity. Building on this design, an enhanced relation graph 
emphasizes high-confidence relational patterns to improve entity representations. In addition, a relation-guided gated graph 
attention mechanism is proposed to differentiate semantic edge types and adaptively regulate cross-layer message passing, 
improving robustness to noise and long-tail relations. Hard negative sampling and uncertainty-based weighting are employed 
to stabilize multi-task optimization. Experiments on two standard document-level benchmarks demonstrate that our approach 
achieves state-of-the-art performance, validating its effectiveness in relational reasoning and long-document scenarios.

1 Introduction
Document-level Relation Extraction (DocRE) is a core NLP 
task aiming to identify relations between entities at the docu-
ment level. Unlike sentence-level extraction, DocRE requires 
reasoning across multiple sentences, with entities often 
appearing in multiple mentions, necessitating modeling of 
interactions among mentions distributed throughout a docu-
ment (Delaunay et al. 2023).
Although recent studies have achieved notable progress, 
most existing approaches still rely on pre-identified entities 
and mentions, which restricts their applicability and robust-
ness in real-world scenarios. In practice, DocRE inher -
ently involves tightly coupled sub-tasks, including Mention 
Extraction (ME), Coreference Resolution (COREF), and 
Relation Extraction (RE). However, traditional pipeline-
based frameworks perform these sub-tasks sequentially, 
which inevitably leads to error propagation and limits cross-
task information sharing. Even existing joint models often 
handle COREF and RE in loosely connected modules, fail-
ing to explicitly model their structural dependencies (Zhang 
et al. 2023).
To overcome these limitations, we propose a table-fill-
ing based multi-task explicit interaction framework. In our 
framework, mentions are treated as graph nodes, and multi-
ple task-specific graphs—including a dynamic coreference 
graph, a relation graph, and a syntactic graph—are jointly 
constructed. More importantly, we introduce an alignment

2  Related work
With the rapid growth of text from social media, news, and 
scientific literature, document-level relation extraction has 
become a key NLP task. It identifies semantic relations 
between entities in long texts and captures cross-sentence 
interactions essential for understanding complex events, sup-
porting applications such as knowledge graph construction 
and event comprehension.
However, most studies assume entities and mentions are 
pre-identified, which rarely holds in practice where entity 
recognition and relation extraction are tightly coupled. 
Moreover, reliance on predefined relation schemas limits 
scalability to unseen or evolving relation types. These limi-
tations have motivated increasing research interest in joint 
entity and relation extraction, where multiple subtasks are 
modeled in a unified framework to improve robustness and 
practical applicability.
2.1  Generative models
Generative approaches treat document-level entity and 
relation extraction as conditional text generation, decoding 
structured outputs from linearized sequences using pre-
trained Seq2Seq models (Nayak and Ng 2020).
Several generative methods have been proposed. Cabot 
et al. (Cabot and Navigli 2021) introduced REBEL on 
DocRED (Yao et al. 2019), linearizing relational triples 
with BART-large, but retaining only the first mention per 
entity, limiting coverage. Giorgi et al. (Giorgi et al. 2022) 
incorporated all mentions into the decoding sequence, which 
increases sequence length, introduces redundancy, and 
makes the decoding process sensitive to mention ordering, 
while clusters without relations are still ignored. Saini et al. 
(Saini and Nayak 2025) proposed 3G-DocRTE, a three-stage 
framework performing mention extraction, entity clustering, 
and triple generation sequentially.
Despite these advances, generative methods face sev -
eral intrinsic limitations. Their linearized decoding length 
grows rapidly with the number of mentions and relations, 
leading to high inference cost, redundancy, and missing 
predictions in long or densely connected documents. More 
importantly, generative models lack explicit structural con-
straints to enforce cluster consistency and relation complete-
ness, which makes them less suitable for modeling com-
plex multi-hop dependencies. These limitations motivate 
discriminative frameworks that explicitly model structured 
interactions among mentions, entities, and relations. To 
address this, we adopt a discriminative framework explicitly 
modeling entity–relation interactions to improve accuracy 
and robustness.
2.2  Discriminative models
Compared with generative approaches, discriminative mod-
els adopt explicit classification strategies to hierarchically 
model entity mentions, entity clusters, and relations, ena-
bling end-to-end joint extraction of mention recognition, 
coreference resolution, and relation classification.
Eberts and Ulges (Eberts and Ulges 2021) proposed 
JEREX, a three-stage framework addressing mention extrac-
tion, coreference resolution, and relational triple extraction, 
supporting both pipeline and joint training. It first identifies 
mentions via span classification, clusters them with pair -
wise classification, and finally predicts relations between 
entity clusters. Xu and Choi (Xu and Choi 2022) further 
introduced a graph compatibility mechanism to encour -
age mutual enhancement between coreference and relation 
decisions, enabling implicit cross-task interaction through

4  Methodology
We propose a Multi-Task Explicit Interaction model for 
document-level joint entity and relation extraction (MTEI). 
The model first encodes documents with a pretrained lan-
guage model to capture contextual semantics and employs 
BIO tagging to extract entity mentions, serving as the basis 
for coreference resolution and relation extraction. Based on 
the extracted mentions, a unified table-filling framework is 
used to jointly model COREF and RE.
To systematically integrate heterogeneous structural signals, 
we construct five complementary mention-level graphs, includ-
ing a coreference graph, a relation graph, an enhanced relation 
graph, and a syntactic graph. These graphs capture task-specific 
and structural dependencies within and across sentences. In 
addition, we introduce a semantic alignment graph that explicitly 
models structured cross-task dependencies between COREF and 
RE. Unlike prior joint models that rely on implicit feature fusion, 
the alignment graph introduces directed and task-aware edges 
to establish explicit information pathways between the two sub-
tasks, enabling regulated bidirectional information propagation. 
Based on these five complementary subgraphs, we regard them 
as heterogeneous edge types and employ a relation-guided graph 
network to model fine-grained task dependencies and enhance 
cross-task information sharing. Finally, the refined mention and 
contextual representations are used to predict COREF and RE 
scores. The overall architecture is illustrated in Fig. 1, where 
the left part corresponds to the mention extractor module, and 
the right part represents the table-filling module for coreference 
resolution and relation extraction. The detailed structures of 
these components are shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively.

4.1  Mention extractor
The core objective of Mention Extraction (ME) is to iden-
tify all entity mentions in a document. As the fundamental 
subtask of document-level joint entity and relation extrac-
tion, ME requires accurate boundary detection and contex-
tual understanding to address challenges such as ambiguous 
mention spans and long-range contextual dependencies in 
lengthy documents.
Following Zhang et al. (Zhang et al. 2023), the proposed 
model takes a multi-sentence document as input, tokenizes 
and encodes it using a pretrained language model, and out-
puts predicted mentions through a BIO tagging scheme, 
where each token is labeled as the beginning, inside, or 
outside of a mention. Decoding the predicted labels yields 
precise mention spans, thereby providing structured and reli-
able inputs for subsequent coreference resolution and rela-
tion extraction, as illustrated in Fig. 2.
4.2  Table filling for coreference resolution 
and relation extraction
Coreference Resolution identifies all mentions referring 
to the same entity within a document. It models semantic 
associations and contextual dependencies across sentences 
to handle long-range coreference, enhancing entity represen-
tations and providing a consistent foundation for relational 
reasoning.
Relation Extraction identifies semantic relations between 
entity pairs. As a core module of document-level joint 
extraction, it captures inter-entity semantics and contex-
tual cues, addressing long-range relations and multi-label 
classification.
To jointly learn coreference resolution and relation 
extraction, we propose an explicit interaction framework 
that enhances cross-task information sharing and improves 
overall extraction performance. The framework constructs 
an alignment graph, incorporates relation-level gating, and 
introduces a joint optimization objective to better capture 
semantic dependencies among mentions and distinguish 
different edge types during graph modeling. This design 
enables more accurate and coherent extraction in complex 
documents, as illustrated in Fig. 3.

Alignment Graph.  Despite the above graph structures, 
information propagation across the coreference resolution 
and relation extraction subtasks remains indirect. To explic-
itly regulate cross-task information flow, we construct a 
semantic alignment graph G ali to encode structured depend-
encies between the two subtasks. We first select high-confi-
dence coreference mention pairs from G cr whose coreference 
probabilities exceed a threshold ρcr . For each mention, an 
averaged relation distribution is computed from G re . We then 
adopt cosine similarity to measure the semantic proximity 
between the relation distributions of mention pairs in the 
relation space. Cosine similarity is adopted due to its scale 
invariance and robustness to distribution smoothing, and has 
been shown to be effective in capturing semantic consistency 
between high dimensional distributional representations 
in prior work (Arora et al. (Arora et al. 2017); Ethayarajh 
(Ethayarajh 2019)). In contrast to divergence based metrics 
such as KL divergence, cosine similarity focuses on direc-
tional agreement between distributions rather than absolute 
probability calibration, which better matches our objective 
of modeling relational semantic consistency across mentions 
(Pereyra et al. (Pereyra et al. 2017)). If both the coreference 
confidence exceeds ρcr and the relation distribution similar-
ity exceeds a threshold ρre , a directed edge m i→m j is added 
to G ali . This dual threshold filtering strategy ensures that 
only mention pairs that are both coreferentially plausible 
and semantically consistent in the relation space are con-
nected, thereby enforcing explicit structural constraints on 
cross-task information propagation. The resulting alignment 
graph integrates complementary signals from both subtasks 
and facilitates fine grained joint modeling and interactive 
reasoning.

5.1  Datasets
Our model is evaluated on widely used document-level rela-
tion extraction datasets: DocRED(Yao et al. 2019) and Re-
DocRED(Tan et al. 2022).
DocRED contains over 10,000 Wikipedia documents and 
97 relation types (including NA). The task requires extract-
ing cross-sentence relations with complex semantic reason-
ing and long-distance dependencies. The dataset provides 
annotations for multiple entity categories (e.g., persons, 
locations, organizations) and diverse relation types.
Re-DocRED is a re-annotated extension of DocRED that 
enriches relation facts and adds coreference information.

5.3  Baseline joint extraction models
We compare MTEI against several baseline joint extraction 
models. Earlier methods typically employed LSTMs for con-
textual encoding, whereas recent approaches rely on fine-
tuned PLMs to capture richer semantics. We train our model 
on DocRED and Re-DocRED and benchmark it against the 
following models:
KB-IE (2021) (Verlinden et al. 2021): Integrates back -
ground knowledge from Wikipedia and Wikidata into a 
joint information extraction framework.
Xu and Choi (2022) (Xu and Choi 2022): Implements a 
standard pipeline system with a shared encoder and joint 
loss, and further proposes JointM + GPGC, which enables 
unidirectional information flow from RE to COREF.
JEREX (2021) (Eberts and Ulges 2021): Enhances rela-
tion extraction through multi-instance learning.
seq2rel (2022) (Giorgi et al. 2022): A sequence-to-
sequence model equipped with a copy mechanism.
TableFiller (2023) (Zhang et al. 2023): Uses a simple 
heuristic decoding strategy with a mention extractor, a 
biaffine encoder, and a classifier.
TAG (2023) (Zhang et al. 2023): A table-filling frame-
work that improves information sharing via a table-to-
graph generation mechanism.
3G-DocRTE (2025)  (Saini and Nayak 2025 ): Unifies 
document-level sub-tasks through a linearization strat-
egy, sequentially generating mentions, normalizing them 
into entity clusters, and extracting relation triples via lin-
earized decoding.
5.4  Experimental results
In document-level joint extraction, we follow the standard 
evaluation protocol widely adopted in prior work. The over-
all evaluation covers three subtasks, each associated with 
specific metrics. To avoid confusion caused by multiple 
F1-based scores, we explicitly clarify all evaluation metrics 
and notations used throughout this section, as summarized 
in Table 4.
Specifically, mention extraction (ME) is evaluated using 
mention-level F1. Coreference resolution (COREF) perfor -

(RE) is evaluated using entity-level F1, as well as Ign F1,

ing set.

5.5  Ablation experimental results and analysis
To assess the impact of each component, we perform abla-
tion studies on DocRED and Re-DocRED using different 
PLMs, as shown in Tables 7, 8 and 9.
Tables 7, 8 and 9  report systematic ablation studies on 
DocRED and Re-DocRED under different PLM settings, 
examining key components of our model, including the 
alignment graph (align), enhanced RE graph (enhanced), 
syntactic graph(syn), relation-guided graph network 
(R-GCN), uncertainty-aware weighting (un-wei), and 
hard negative sampling (hard neg). We further conduct 
combined ablations by simultaneously removing both the 
enhanced and alignment modules (w/o enhanced + align), 
as well as removing all auxiliary components (w/o all), in 
order to assess the degree of mutual dependency and com-
plementarity among different modules. Overall, removing 
any individual component leads to consistent performance 
degradation in COREF F1, RE F1, and RE Ign F1 across 
all datasets and PLM backbones, demonstrating that each 
module contributes positively and complementarily to the 
joint extraction framework.
Among all ablations, removing the alignment graph 
causes the most severe performance drop, indicating its 
critical role in explicitly modeling semantic interactions 
between coreference resolution and relation extraction. By 
constructing alignment structures over mention-level repre-
sentations, this module jointly optimizes entity consistency 
and relational reasoning. Its removal disrupts cross-task 
information flow, allowing coreference errors to propagate 
into relation prediction, thereby substantially degrading joint 
performance. These results confirm the necessity and effec-
tiveness of explicit cross-task dependency modeling.
Notably, the observed performance degradation is closely 
linked to the mitigation of error propagation across tasks. 
In document-level joint extraction, errors introduced at the 
mention extraction or coreference resolution stage can eas-
ily propagate into relation extraction, as inaccurate mention 
boundaries or incorrect coreference links distort the rela-
tional context of entity pairs. This cascading effect is clearly 
seen in the ablation result from the w/o align setting, where 
the absence of the alignment graph leads to substantial drops 
in both COREF and RE F1 scores across all datasets and 
PLM backbones (Tables  7, 8 and 9 ). Without the explicit 
structural constraints provided by the alignment graph, erro-
neous coreference signals can propagate unchecked into rela-
tion reasoning, thereby degrading performance.
In contrast, the alignment graph explicitly couples men-
tion-level coreference representations with relation-level 
semantic distributions, allowing the model to selectively 
propagate reliable cross-task information while suppress-
ing noisy or inconsistent signals. This structured interaction 
prevents error cascades at the interface between COREF and 
RE, stabilizing downstream relation prediction. By enforc-
ing cross-task structural dependencies, the alignment graph 
ensures that errors in one subtask do not disproportionately 
affect the other, providing a robust mechanism for mitigating 
error propagation.
The error-mitigation role of the alignment graph is fur -
ther evidenced by the w/o enhanced + align setting, where 
jointly removing both modules results in significantly larger 
performance drops compared to removing either component 
individually. This observation underscores the critical role 
of the alignment graph in preventing compounded errors 
during joint inference. Without alignment supervision, even 
the enhanced relational evidence alone is insufficient to com-
pensate for upstream coreference noise, further validating 
the importance of explicit cross-task alignment.
Removing any auxiliary component consistently leads to 
stable degradation in joint extraction performance, indicat-
ing that these modules play complementary roles within the 
overall framework. Specifically, eliminating the syntactic 
graph results in noticeable yet relatively moderate perfor -
mance drops across all experimental settings, suggesting 
that syntactic structures provide beneficial local dependency 
constraints for mention representation modeling and cross-
sentence reasoning, thereby stabilizing the joint modeling 
process. Removing the relation-guided graph network also 
causes performance degradation, confirming the importance 
of edge-type–aware information propagation for capturing 
fine-grained dependencies across heterogeneous struc-
tural views. The enhanced RE graph plays a critical role in 
improving the robustness of relation extraction by supple-
menting long-distance relational evidence and alleviating 
relational sparsity, and its removal consistently weakens both 
RE F1 and Ign F1. Meanwhile, uncertainty-aware weight-
ing contributes to dynamically balancing task difficulty 
during multi-task optimization, and hard negative sampling 
enhances discrimination among semantically similar nega-
tive relations. Notably, simultaneously removing both the 
enhanced RE graph and the alignment module results in sub-
stantially larger performance deterioration than removing 
either component alone, revealing strong complementarity 
between explicit cross-task alignment modeling and rela-
tional enhancement mechanisms, and further verifying the

5.6  Similarity analysis experiment
To further validate that the alignment graph proposed in 
Section 4.3 indeed connects semantically similar mention 
pairs, we conduct a node representation similarity analy -
sis. Specifically, the mean of each node’s relation vectors is 
used as its representation, and cosine similarity is computed 
between all node pairs. On the dev set, similarity distribu-
tions for align = 1 and align = 0 mention pairs are visual-
ized using kernel density estimation curves and box plots, 
as shown in Figs. 4 and 5.
As shown in Fig.  4, the results exhibit a clear and con-
sistent pattern. The similarity distribution of align = 1 pairs 
shows a sharp peak near 1.0, indicating that most aligned 
mention pairs are highly consistent in the embedding space. 
In contrast, align = 0 pairs display a flatter distribution shifted 
toward lower similarity values with a heavier tail, reflecting 
greater semantic diversity and noise. Figure 5 further quan-
tifies this gap: align = 1 pairs achieve a higher median and 
interquartile range with a smaller overall spread, demonstrat-
ing both higher similarity and stronger internal consistency. 
This trend remains stable across different sample sizes, sug-
gesting that the observed separation is not incidental.
Overall, the similarity analysis validates the alignment 
graph design: it preferentially links semantically similar 
mentions, yielding more reliable paths for coreference aggre-
gation and relation propagation. Consistent with ablation 
results, removing this graph causes the largest performance 
drop, highlighting its key role in reducing error propagation, 
and improving relation extraction.
5.7  Enhanced graph threshold selection 
experiment
To assess the effect of the edge filtering threshold 
in the enhanced RE graph, we vary the threshold /u1D70C
∈{0.1,0.2,…,0.9} while keeping all other settings fixed. 
Our model is trained and evaluated at each value, and F1/
Ign F1 on the development sets are reported and plotted as 
line charts in Fig. 6.
The results indicate that the edge filtering threshold sub-
stantially affects the structural quality of the enhanced rela-
tion graph. At low thresholds, the graph becomes dense, 
providing rich cross-mention interactions but introducing 
many low-confidence edges, which limits performance gains 
and causes instability. As the threshold increases to a mod-
erate range, noisy edges are gradually removed, leading to 
improved edge quality and a consistent performance increase 
on both development and test sets. At higher thresholds, per-
formance reaches optimal or near-optimal levels, suggesting 
a favorable trade-off between connectivity and noise reduc-
tion that better supports relation inference. However, when 
the threshold further increases to 0.9, the graph becomes 
overly sparse, excessively pruning weak yet informative 
interactions and resulting in performance degradation.
Overall, these results demonstrate that appropriate thresh-
old selection is critical to the effectiveness of the enhanced 
relation graph. Under our setting, ρ = 0.8 achieves a strong 
balance between peak performance and generalization sta-
bility, and is therefore adopted as the default value, provid-
ing empirical support for the hyperparameter choice of the 
enhanced RE graph.

5.9  Weight visualization experiment
To further assess the effectiveness of the uncertainty-
based weighting mechanism in multi-task joint optimi-
zation, we visualize the task weight dynamics during 
training. The four subtasks—coreference table predic -
tion, relation table prediction, coreference classification, 
and relation classification—differ in learning difficulty 
and convergence speed. Uniform weighting may there-
fore induce gradient conflicts and degrade overall per -
formance. To examine how uncertainty weighting facili-
tates cross-task coordination, we track the evolution of 
the learnable uncertainty parameters log σ for each task 
and visualize the corresponding weight trajectories and 
heatmaps, as shown in Fig.  9.
From the dynamic weight visualizations, we observe 
that uncertainty-based weighting provides effective 
adaptive regulation during multi-task joint learning. 
Specifically, the learnable parameters log σ for all four 
subtasks exhibit a consistent downward trend, indicat-
ing progressively reduced estimated prediction variance 
and decreasing training uncertainty as learning proceeds. 
Correspondingly, the task weights, which are inversely 
related to log σ, increase overall, showing that the model 
gradually allocates more emphasis to tasks with lower 
uncertainty and more stable learning, thereby enabling 
efficient distribution of optimization capacity.
Notably, the relation extraction (RE) task displays a dis-
tinct weighting pattern compared with the other subtasks. 
In the early training stage, its log σ temporarily increases 
rather than decreases, implying heightened uncertainty in 
relation prediction. This behavior reflects the greater dif-
ficulty and instability of document-level relation reasoning; 
assigning it a large weight too early could amplify noisy 
gradients and hinder joint optimization. The model therefore 
adaptively suppresses its influence by increasing log σ. As 
training progresses, RE increasingly benefits from corefer -
ence signals and table-structured predictions, leading to a 
marked reduction in uncertainty and a gradual rise in its 
weight, which stabilizes in later stages. This trend indicates 
that the model dynamically adjusts its reliance on relation 
reasoning—down-weighting it initially to ensure stability 
and strengthening it once reliable learning signals emerge.
The weight heatmaps further illustrate both stability and 
differentiation in weight evolution. Overall, task weights 
converge from dispersed distributions, suggesting that 
uncertainty weighting effectively mitigates gradient noise 
across tasks. Meanwhile, RE exhibits larger spatial fluctua-
tions than the others, consistent with its stronger dependence 
on contextual and structural cues in document-level infer -
ence and its greater need for dynamic adjustment.
In summary, uncertainty-based weighting enables adap-
tive optimization according to task difficulty and training 
dynamics. It alleviates gradient conflicts and imbalance 
across tasks while progressively increasing emphasis on 
relation extraction in later stages, thereby enhancing cross-
task information integration and improving overall perfor -
mance and generalization in document-level joint entity and 
relation extraction.
6  Conclusions
This paper proposes a table-filling based multi-task 
explicit interaction framework for document-level joint 
entity and relation extraction. The core contribution is 
the introduction of an alignment graph that explicitly 
models structured dependencies between COREF and 
RE, enabling regulated bidirectional information flow 
across subtasks and improving reasoning consistency in 
long and structurally complex documents. An enhanced 
RE graph is introduced to prioritize high-confidence rela-
tion features, further improving entity representations. 
To strengthen the graph network’s ability to differentiate 
multi-type semantic edges, a relation-guided gated graph 
attention mechanism is employed for adaptive informa-
tion modulation. Additionally, integrating hard negative 
sampling with uncertainty-based weighting effectively 
mitigates noise in cross-task information propagation, 
improving training stability and multi-task coordination. 
Extensive experiments on standard DocRE benchmarks 
demonstrate that the proposed model achieves state-of-
the-art overall performance and key metrics, validating 
its effectiveness in improving document-level relation 
extraction accuracy and handling long-document scenar -
ios. Future work will explore adaptive alignment strate -
gies and richer structural priors to further enhance scal-
ability and generalization in complex document contexts.
7  Limitations
Our framework introduces additional computational over -
head due to multi-graph construction and propagation, 
which may limit scalability for extremely long documents 
with many mentions. Moreover, the model depends on suf-
ficient supervision to learn stable cross-task interactions. In 
low-resource settings, sparse or noisy coreference and rela-
tion annotations may weaken the effectiveness of the align-
ment and enhanced relation graphs. Improving scalability 
and robustness under such conditions remains an important 
direction for future work.
